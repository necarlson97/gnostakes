Gnostakes
Drink deeply, alchemist. Perhaps enlightenment rests with the dregs of this noxious flask.
In Gnostakes, you play a medieval mystic sojourner, imbibing vials full of chemicals and crystals.
Prep
Each player gets:
5 Potency dice with the faces: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
5 Elemental dice with the faces:Earth, Earth, Water, Water, Fire, Air
a flat area for rolling dice - your laboratory
an area for committing dice - your glassworks
and an area for keeping active cards -your cadre of followers and fiends
7 starting teeth (and 0 starting keys)

Take the deck of villager cards, shuffle, and place the deck (face down) in the middle of the table.Have a space for the incinerated pyre of discarded villager cards next to it.

In the center of the table is the Town Square sheet where you recruit new villagers to your cadre.
This sheet has 4 places to lay out cards, and descriptions for the types of scoring hands that will let your claim them.

Draw and lay out 4 villager cards for the 4 slots in the Town Square.
Swig Phase
Concoct strange potions, and drink them down:
roll and commit dice.

On swig start, all of your dice are "ready to be rolled".Roll them, and for each die, chose to:
Commit: face is kept to be scored during Scry
Discard: set aside, not counted during scoring
Leave Ready: to be rolled again this Swig

From your pool of rolled dice, choose at least 5 to commit or discard.Note some villagers can manipulate die face values, allow them to be uncommitted, etc.
Take all uncommitted dice, and roll them again. Again, you must choose at least 5 to commit or discard. Or all, if there were < 5 remaining.

You keep rolling, committing/discarding some, and rolling again - until you have no more ready dice.
Scry Phase
Access the depths, and spill your prophecy to recruit new followers: score your hand, buy new cards.

View the villager cards on offer in the Town Square.View your committed dice, and arrange your dice into the sets that they score.

You can score multiple sets, but each die can only belong to one set. E.g.: WWW and 123 are two scoring sets.But 11123 is not 'Triplet and Three Straight'- you'd have to choose one.

There are 4 villagers on offer:
Requested with Three Straight or 3 Tokens
Requested with Two Color-Pairs or 3 Tokens
Requested with Triplet or 3 Tokens
Requested with Four of a Color or 3 Tokens
More powerful sets allow you to request any one of the 4:Four Straight, Three Ones, All Colors, Four Pairs
And the most powerful allow you request two of the 4:Five Straight, Quadruplet, Four Air, Five of a Color

If only you requested that villager, you take the card, and add them to your cadre.
Any time you take a card, replace it with a new draw.

However, if players are requesting the same villager, you can wager an amount of tokens:
Grab some amount of your tokens, holding them in your fist. Cover/hide your other tokens.
Simultaneously open to reveal your wager.
Winner is whomever wagered the most.Raw total, ignoring element types.
Winner spends those tokens, and wins the card.The loser(s) keeps their wager.If there is a tie, you both edit your wager, adding or removing, then reveal again. If players tie with all tokens wagered, roll for it.

Instead of requesting the same villager, you may allow your opponent to take the card, then see if you want to take whatever new villager replaces them.

Once players have all taken their villagers:
Move all villager cards one slot to the left. The leftmost card is incinerated, the rightmost card is a new draw.

Incinerated villagers are discarded, placed face down in the center of the table.
They are not redrawn in the town square.
If the village deck is ever empty, the game is over.
Scheme Phase
Lord over your followers, tinker in your laboratory, prepare the next brew: Flip cards, swap die faces.

Look at your villagers, and for each, decide which ability is activated (rightside-up) and which is deactivated (upside-down).
You (usually) only invert cards during Scheme.

You can (by default) only have 4 villagers - if you acquired more, incinerate down to 4.

Some villagers will allow you to open up and edit the faces on your dice - now is the time to do so.
When all players are ready, proceed to the next Swig Phase. Repeat, until a winner is enlightened.
Enlightenment
Keys are, well, key to winning. They are acquired with villager abilities - pay special attention to villagers that grant them.

There are 3 conditions that end the game:
Edification: any player garners 7 keys
Putrescence: any player loses all their teeth
Conflagration: the Village Deck has run out
Once one of these conditions have been met, the game is over at the end of the next Scheme Phase.

Any players with no teeth die in ruined bodies.Among those still living - the player with the most keys is enlightened, and wins the game!

It is possible to trigger Putrescence, but gain teeth during the final turn and win the game.
Question Solvers
When you invert a villager card, you do keep any buried tokens, the newly active ability may even allow you to spend them.

Relinquished cards that get attached to opponents - those are no longer yours: you cannot invert them, they do not count against your max villager total, etc.Typically, the opponent removes them by incinerating their attached card (e.g, by having a full cadre and requesting a new villager).

When you manually "rotate a die to any face" - this does retrigger on-roll. E.g., if you want to trigger a villagers "On rolling an F…" - you can do that by manually rotating a die to an F.

If a card says "spend all tokens of 1 type" - you cannot 'spend' a token you have 0 of.

During Scry, if your committed dice satisfy a set - you have "scored" that set, like it or not. So if you don't want to trigger an ability that says "On scoring four fire…" - be careful not to commit dice that satisfy 'four fire' - or to specifically score those dice as conflicting sets.E.g.: you could roll: ◆◆◆◆◆◆but score it as Two Color-Pairs: ◆◆◆◆instead of Four of a Kind (fire).Keep that in mind for "If you score nothing…".
Just because you scored a set, doesn't mean you need to request a villager, it is fine to request nothing.

'An additional' indicates that the gain/loss does not retrigger itself infinitely. E.g.:"When you incinerate a card… incinerate an additional card" or "When you gain a key: … gain an additional key." - those do not infinitely loop forever.

"Incinerate a card" or "Invert a card" means one of your own cards, a card in your cadre - not an opponentsunless explicitly stated.

'Undiscard' is like a type of reroll - you can discard dice you don't like to meet the "choose at least 5 to commit or discard" limit - then undiscard them to immediately have them ready to roll again. 

A die has to be in a scoring set to be considered "scored'. I.e.: a committed die that doesn't end up part of any set is not "scored".

You can't gain more than 7 teeth.
